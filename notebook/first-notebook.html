How an entry begins

Write when there is something to record. Each entry opens by saying where it was written: in the year, under the Moon, and on the Earth.

  1. The Sun. Its sign and degree. This is the date. The year begins at 1º ♈︎.
  2. The Moon. Its sign and degree, then its phase.
  3. The year. Anno Mundi, the common year, or both.
  4. The place. Latitude and longitude. Written once, and again after a move.
  5. Solar time. Sunrise, noon and sunset for that place, looked up. Solar noon and clock noon are rarely the same moment.
  6. The quarter. Spring, Summer, Autumn or Winter.
  7. Train time. The calendar date, small, for meeting the world.

Then write. A line is an entry. A page is an entry.

From a chart to the journal. Charts and almanacs measure from zero: 9°52′ ♏︎. The journal counts the way a calendar does, first, second, third. Leave the minutes and add one: 9°52′ is in the 10th degree, written 10º ♏︎. A chart's 0° is the journal's 1º.

Finding the phase

Sun: ♎︎ ends at 210º, and 10 more: 220º Moon: ♉︎ ends at 60º, and 13 more: 73º Moon − Sun: 73 − 220 falls below zero, so add 360: 213º, which is full.

Or around the circle

Count the signs from the Sun on to the Moon. From 10º ♏︎ to 10º ♊︎ is seven signs: 210º The Moon is at 13º, three further: 213º, full.

One sign is 30º, and the twelve make 360º. To place a day in the whole year, take where the sign before it ends and add the degree: 10º ♏︎ is 210 and 10, the 220th degree. Moon minus Sun gives the phase. Below zero, add 360. Or count the signs around the circle from the Sun on to the Moon, 30º for each.
